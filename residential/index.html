Upfront Pricing

Honest, Upfront Pricing

Before we pick up a wrench, we figure out exactly what's wrong. Our technicians check your heater's age and gallon capacity, the burner or elements, the relief valve, the venting, and any rust or seepage around the fittings. After that you get a straightforward recommendation and the full price up front, whether the job is swapping a thermocouple or replacing the whole unit. No hidden fees, no surprise add-ons.

Many water heater problems stay hidden until a failure is well underway. Sediment builds up on the bottom of the tank, the anode rod quietly dissolves, and the steel underneath starts to rust from the inside with no outward warning. If you notice rusty hot water, rumbling or popping sounds, a rotten-egg smell, hot water that runs out sooner than it used to, or moisture around the base, schedule an inspection sooner rather than later. An early diagnosis usually keeps a fixable issue from becoming a replacement.

How old is your water heater? The serial number on the label usually tells us. Once a tank passes the 10-year mark, it's worth scheduling a replacement before the current one forces the issue. For reference, a 40 to 50 gallon gas tank typically runs $1,400–$2,800 installed, and an electric tank typically runs $1,100–$2,400, depending on venting, code updates, and access. We will tell you plainly whether yours has years left or is nearing the end.

Maintenance Plans

Maintenance Plans for Vallejo Homes

Routine maintenance is the most reliable way to keep hot water flowing all year. Regular service prevents failures before they start, flushes out mineral sediment, and catches small faults while they're still cheap to fix. At Vallejo Water Heater, every maintenance visit is built around your specific heater, covering a full tank flush, an anode rod inspection, a test of the temperature and pressure relief valve, a burner and flue check on gas units, element and thermostat testing on electric units, and a thermostat setting check.

We offer flexible maintenance options for homeowners, with annual service for most tanks and more frequent flushing where the water is hard. This is especially important where mineral content is high, where sediment insulates the tank bottom, makes the burner work harder, and wears out the tank. Each home is assessed individually, and the plan is tailored to your heater's age, your household's hot water demands, and your local water.

The payoff shows up faster than most people expect. A tank without a sediment crust transfers heat more efficiently, a replaced anode rod keeps corrosion off the tank walls, and a tested relief valve is ready to do its job if pressure ever climbs too high. Next to the cost of an early replacement, regular maintenance is money well spent.

Sized for Your Household

The Right Size Water Heater for Your Home

Proper sizing matters more than most homeowners realize. Before quoting a new heater, Vallejo Water Heater looks at the details that drive your household's peak demand: the number of people and bathrooms, showers that run back to back, laundry and dishwasher timing, and how cold the water entering the house gets. The result is a heater with enough first-hour capacity or GPM to handle your busiest hour without paying to heat water you'll never use.

We walk you through the options for every home, especially growing families with rising hot water demands. A 40-gallon tank that served a couple rarely holds out once kids are showering every morning, and a tankless water heater can deliver steady hot water so long as it's sized for the flow. If you're weighing tank against tankless, or gas against a heat pump model, our technician will lay out the honest trade-offs: upfront cost, operating cost, gas line and venting requirements, electrical capacity, and space, so you can pick what fits your home and budget.

Frequent Failures

The Water Heater Repairs Local Homes Need Most

Homes around Vallejo run into a familiar set of water heater failures, and each one calls for a different fix. Vallejo Water Heater handles them all: no hot water, water that only gets lukewarm, pilots that won't stay lit, electric tanks that trip the breaker, rumbling tanks full of sediment, rusty or smelly water, leaking relief valves, and tankless units flashing error codes. Our technicians identify the exact fault and target the repair to the component that actually failed.

Some problems are just a nuisance, while others pose real risks. A burned-out element simply leaves you with lukewarm water, but a relief valve that keeps discharging is warning you that pressure or temperature in the tank is running too high, and a thermostat set above 120°F can scald a child in seconds. If you ever smell gas near the heater, leave the house and call your gas utility from outside before you call us. We match the fix to the failure: elements and thermostats on electric tanks, thermocouples, flame sensors, and gas valves on gas units, a thermal expansion tank where pressure is spiking, and leak repair on fittings, drain valves, and relief lines.
