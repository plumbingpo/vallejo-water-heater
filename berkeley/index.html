How Berkeley Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

Many early failures start with maintenance that never happened, and the habits that prevent them are simple. Drain and flush the tank every year to clear sediment, get the anode rod checked every few years, and test the relief valve each year so it doesn't seize. Keep the temperature near 120 degrees to reduce scald risk and slow scale buildup. For a tankless heater in a hard-water area, plan on descaling once a year so the heat exchanger stays clean.

Age matters too. Most tank water heaters last 8 to 12 years, tankless units often reach 20 with regular descaling, and heat pump models usually last 10 to 15. If your heater is past that window and showing warning signs like discolored hot water, rumbling during heating, dampness under the tank, or hot water that runs out sooner than it used to, a planned replacement is nearly always less expensive and much easier on you than an emergency one with water on the floor.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to high water pressure or thermal expansion with nowhere to go, both inexpensive fixes. The same drip left alone for a season can keep stressing the tank until the tank itself fails, and a small repair becomes a full replacement plus water damage. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and ruined floors.
