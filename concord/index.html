Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Concord

Routine maintenance is the difference between a water heater that lasts and one that floods the garage in year seven. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: a yearly flush, an anode inspection every few years, a quick relief valve test once a year, a thermostat set near 120 degrees, and yearly descaling for any tankless unit on hard water, so small issues get caught early.

Take a look at the age of your water heater. The serial number on the label usually includes the month and year it was built, and its age tells you a lot. Most tank models reach 8 to 12 years, heat pump models 10 to 15, and regularly descaled tankless units about 20. If your heater is past that range and also shows rusty water, rumbling, or moisture at the base, it's a strong candidate for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than a rushed emergency install once the tank fails.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to high water pressure or thermal expansion with nowhere to go, both modest fixes. The same drip ignored for months can wear out the valve and stress the tank until a seam gives way, and a small repair becomes a full replacement plus water damage. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and ruined floors.
