Keeping Your Richmond Water Heater Running Longer

A little routine care makes all the difference between a water heater that lasts and one that floods the garage in year seven. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: a yearly flush, an anode rod check every three to five years, a quick relief valve test once a year, a temperature setting around 120 degrees, and yearly descaling for any tankless unit on hard water, so problems are found while they're still inexpensive.

The age of your heater matters just as much. With regular service, a standard tank typically lasts 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts acting up, like rust-colored water, popping noises, or a damp spot around the base, it's smart to start planning a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule costs less and is far less stressful than an emergency swap with water spreading across the floor.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to excess pressure or a missing expansion tank, both modest fixes. The same drip ignored for months can wear out the valve and stress the tank until a seam gives way, and a small repair becomes a full replacement and a cleanup. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and cold showers.

What Goes Wrong With Local Water Heaters

A water heater in Richmond rarely fails out of nowhere. Watch for a popping or rumbling sound while the tank heats, brownish hot water, a faint rotten-egg smell, moisture around the base, or hot water that runs out a little sooner each month. Those point to sediment buildup, a spent anode rod, a failing element, or corrosion starting at the bottom seam, and caught early, most of them mean a modest repair or a replacement on your schedule. Ignored, they end in a failed tank and water on the floor. Pay close attention to a relief valve that drips or discharges: it's the tank telling you pressure or temperature is running too high, and it should be checked promptly. We find the root cause instead of treating symptoms, so the small problem never gets the chance to grow.
