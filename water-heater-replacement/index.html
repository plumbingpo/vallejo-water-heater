How do you know it is time to replace a water heater rather than repair it? Start with the age. Tanks tend to wear out between eight and twelve years, and the serial number on the label dates yours precisely. The symptoms come next. A leak from the tank itself, rusty water from the hot side only, and loud sediment noise all point to a tank near the end. Parts like thermocouples, elements, and relief valves can be replaced on almost any heater, but a rusted-through tank cannot, and homeowners in Vallejo, CA tend to learn that the hard way, with a wet floor.

A new water heater should fit the way your household uses hot water today, not the way it did when the old one went in. That is why Vallejo Water Heater starts with a few questions: who showers when, whether there is a big soaking tub, and how many fixtures run at the same time. For tank heaters we compare first-hour ratings; for tankless, we size to the gallons per minute you need at the temperature rise your incoming water demands in the coldest months. For a heat pump model, we check space and airflow, because it draws its heat from the surrounding room. We also confirm your gas line, vent, and electrical panel can handle the new unit, so there are no surprises on installation day.

On installation day, every step gets verified. The old heater is drained and carried out, the new one is set level, and fresh supply connectors and a working shutoff go in. Warning Signs to Watch For

Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Loud rumbling or popping that flushing no longer fixes
  • A heater older than most tanks last, usually eight to twelve years
  • Higher gas or electric bills for less hot water
  • Rust flaking from the tank jacket or its connections
  • Repeated service calls on the same aging heater
  • Discolored hot water, a sign the tank is corroding inside
  • Water leaking from the bottom or body of the tank
  • A household that has outgrown the tank's capacity

Why This Job Calls for a Pro

Start with the venting. A gas water heater's exhaust contains carbon monoxide, and a vent that is undersized, poorly pitched, or loosely connected can let it spill back into your home. Add a gas connection that has to be leak-free, an electrical circuit that has to match the new heater's load, and a relief valve that has to carry the right rating with a discharge line that is never capped or undersized, and the details pile up quickly. Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • Avoid running the tank hotter than about 120 degrees unless there is a specific need
  • Flush the new tank once or twice a year to keep sediment from building up
  • Clean the air filter on a heat pump water heater every few months
  • Write the install date on the tank so you know when to plan the next replacement
  • Keep household water pressure in a safe range to protect the tank and its fittings
  • Tankless units need a yearly descaling, especially where the water is hard
  • A fresh anode rod at the right time is the cheapest way to extend tank life
